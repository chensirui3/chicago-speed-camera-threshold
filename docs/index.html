Did lowering the speed-camera threshold to 6 mph reduce crashes?

On March 1, 2021, Chicago's cameras began ticketing drivers going 6 mph over the limit instead of about 10. Violations at the same cameras jumped almost fivefold. Pick any version of the model below, or click a site on the map, and check the answer yourself.

How to read this

  • Treated sites are 51 park-zone camera sites that ran continuously from before 2018. School-zone cameras went dark during remote learning and came back in early 2021, so they can't be separated from the threshold change and are left out.
  • Future camera sites are places the city later chose for cameras (live 2023–2026), with no camera within 500 m during 2018–2022. Red-light intersections are a second, independent comparison group.
  • The model is a Poisson regression of monthly crash counts with site and month fixed effects and standard errors clustered by site. Crashes are counted within the radius you pick. January and February 2021, the warning period, are left out.
  • Site charts show raw counts within 1/8 mile, not model output. Each site's own numbers are noisy; the estimate above pools all of them.

Data: City of Chicago Data Portal (traffic crashes, speed camera locations and violations, red-light camera locations), downloaded November 2026. Code: github.com/chensirui3/chicago-speed-camera-threshold, MIT License.
